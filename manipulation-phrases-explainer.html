Naming the Game

6 phrases that expose manipulation — not by accusing, but by naming what's happening out loud.

Before You Speak
Know Your Signal

These phrases aren't for every moment of confusion. They're for a specific internal signal. Learn the difference.

Cognitive Signal

Simple Confusion

You don't fully understand what someone is saying. The information is incomplete or unclear.

Ask a clarifying question. This is not the trigger for these phrases.
Somatic Signal — The Real Trigger

The Felt Mismatch

What they're saying and what you're feeling don't line up. Their words seem fine. Your gut disagrees.

This is the trigger. Something landed before you could name it. Use the phrases here.
CONFUSION IS COGNITIVE  ·  THE MISMATCH IS SOMATIC
When Specifically
4 Moments That Call for a Phrase

Watch for these in real time. They are the body telling you the intent isn't clean.

✂️

You're Editing Yourself

You're adjusting what you say mid-sentence to manage their reaction. The frame already shifted — and you didn't choose it.

🔍

You Feel Like the Problem

You have an urge to over-explain or justify yourself and you can't identify why. Manipulation makes the target feel responsible.

🌀

The Conversation Has Drifted

You came to talk about X. Somehow you're on Y and you can't trace how you got there. That drift was not an accident.

⏱️

Pressure to Decide Now

There's a subtle urgency to commit or respond faster than feels right. That pressure is manufactured. Slow down.

The Tools
6 Phrases That Name the Game

Say one. Then stop talking. Silence does the rest.

Phrase 01
"It seems like you're wanting me to feel ___. Was that your intent?"
You're not accusing. You're checking alignment. Clean intent gets corrected fast. Unclear intent produces a pause that tells you everything.
Use WhenYou feel something that doesn't match their words — pressured, guilty, or warned without a direct statement.
Phrase 02
"Help me understand what reaction you're hoping for here."
Moves the conversation from what was said to what outcome they're after. Forces intent into the open without requiring you to guess.
Use WhenThe words feel strategic. Like they're designed to produce a specific emotional response in you.
Phrase 03
"Before I respond — what are you actually wanting from me right now?"
Either a real request surfaces or the pressure collapses. There's no clean answer to this question when the real answer is hidden.
Use WhenSomeone is running a loyalty pitch or emotional appeal without naming what they actually want from you.
Phrase 04
"I thought we were talking about X — but it feels like we're on Y."
Quiet frame control. You're not accusing them of changing the subject. You're naming the drift and redirecting back to the original structure.
Use WhenThe conversation has shifted away from what you came to discuss and you can't track how it happened.
Phrase 05
"That commitment felt like it might be designed to shift control of the conversation."
Once control is named out loud, it can't stay hidden. This one is direct and should be used when you're confident about what you're sensing.
Use WhenSomeone makes a statement that feels like it's designed to put them in charge of what happens next.
Phrase 06
"That's a clever reframe. It puts you in the reasonable seat and me in the irrational one. I'd rather keep this honest."
Ends the game entirely. You've named the move, the result, and the standard you're holding the conversation to — in one sentence.
Use WhenThey've repositioned your words or reaction so that disagreeing with them makes you look unreasonable.
The Mechanism
Say It. Then Go Quiet.

The phrase names the pattern. Silence creates the pressure for them to respond to it. You've done your part. Let the moment work.

If intent was clean

They Correct You

"Oh no — I didn't mean it that way. Let me be clearer." Fast, easy, no defensiveness.

If intent was unclear

They Backpedal

Sudden over-explanation, subject change, or "you're reading too much into it." The scramble is the signal.

If intent was hidden

They Shift Tactics

The conversation pivots. New pressure appears. The original move gets abandoned. You've exposed it.

"Manipulation only works when intent is invisible. Once it's seen and brought into light — the game is open."

These phrases don't require proof. They don't require confrontation. They require visibility. That's enough.

Why Learn Tools Before You Need Them

Most people learn communication frameworks intellectually — and then freeze when the moment arrives because they've never rehearsed how the tool actually lands. By the time the pressure is real, there's no bandwidth to think and respond well at the same time.

The dry run matters. Walk the phrases through real-world scenarios. Picture the specific person, the specific context, the specific phrase. Build the muscle memory before there's anything at stake.

These phrases also transfer. They're not just for obvious manipulators. They work with coworkers, in relationships, with family — anyone whose intent is unclear in a given moment. You're not building a weapon against one person. You're building a general capacity to name what's happening in real time, without escalating.

That capacity is worth carrying everywhere.
